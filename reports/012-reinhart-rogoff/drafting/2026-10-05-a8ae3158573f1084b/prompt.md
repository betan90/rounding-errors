VERIFIED FACTS:
- RR 2010 (quoted in HAP p.1-2): "median growth rates for countries with public debt over roughly 90 percent of GDP are about one percent lower than otherwise; (mean) growth rates are several percent lower". 20 advanced economies, 1946-2009 sample.
- RR published average real GDP growth by debt/GDP bucket (HAP Table 1): below 30%: 4.1; 30-60: 2.8; 60-90: 2.8; 90 and above: -0.1.
- HAP corrected (Table 3, country-year weighting, all data): 4.2; 3.1; 3.2; 2.2.
- HAP abstract: coding errors, selective exclusion of available data, and unconventional weighting of summary statistics; corrected average above 90% is 2.2 percent, not -0.1.
- Spreadsheet error: RR averaged cells in lines 30 to 44 instead of lines 30 to 49 (HAP fn 5), entirely excluding 5 countries: Australia, Austria, Belgium, Canada, Denmark. HAP: "The omitted countries are selected alphabetically". RR's erratum calls it "a coding error that causes the first five countries in the alphabet to be omitted".
- Exclusions: RR excluded available early years for Australia (1946-1950), New Zealand (1946-1949), Canada (1946-1950). NZ's excluded years in the top bucket grew 7.7, 11.9, -9.9 and 10.8 percent; after exclusion NZ contributes 1 year, 1951, at -7.6 percent.
- Country-years in the above-90% bucket: RR reports 96; HAP finds 110; with the spreadsheet error RR in fact used only 71 (25 years of Belgium dropped in addition to 14 excluded years).
- Weighting: RR averaged each country first, then weighted countries equally. UK: 19 years at 2.4% average; NZ: 1 year at -7.6%; US: 4 years at -2.0%. With 7 countries in the top bucket, NZ's single year got 14.3 percent (one-seventh) of the weight. A transcription error moved NZ's average from -7.6 to -7.9 on the summary sheet (another -0.1 point).
- Exclusion plus country weighting alone: almost -2 percentage points (HAP p.10, "a very substantial 1.9 percentage points" p.9). Spreadsheet and transcription errors: an additional -0.4 point.
- Real gap between 60-90 and above-90 buckets: 1.0 point (3.2 less 2.2). RR overstated the gap by 2.3 points, "a factor of nearly two and a half" (HAP p.10).
- HAP p.4: RR 2010a "is the only evidence cited in the 'Paul Ryan Budget' on the consequences of high public debt for economic growth", quoting Ryan's "Path to Prosperity" (2013 p.78): "The study found conclusive empirical evidence that gross debt ... exceeding 90 percent of the economy has a significant negative effect on economic growth." Attribute this as HAP's report of the budget document (we have not fetched the budget itself).
- HAP p.4: RR's website lists 76 high-profile media features; Google Scholar finds more than 500 results citing the paper excluding the authors' own.
- RR erratum (May 5, 2013): thanks HAP "for pointing out an important correction". Lists HAP's 3 points: the coding error, "selective exclusion" for New Zealand, "unconventional weighting of summary statistics". Says HAP is silent on the longer sample back to the 1800s and on emerging markets, and "fails to mention that our median estimates for the postwar sample are very close in magnitude to the averages that they present". Refers to their NYT response of April 26, 2013 (do not quote the NYT; not fetched).
- RR erratum Table 1, postwar, above 90% bucket: published mean -0.1 (blank-cell clean-up alone: 0.0), published median 1.6. "Coding corrected": mean 0.3, median 1.9. Their 60-90% coding-corrected postwar mean: 2.5.
