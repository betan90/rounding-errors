## The incident

Japan's Monthly Labour Survey is a fundamental statistic under the Statistics Act. Its survey plan and published materials said every establishment with 500 or more employees would be surveyed in full. From 2004 the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) sent Tokyo a list of establishments it had sampled, and Tokyo surveyed the list.

For 2018 a full count of Tokyo's 500+ establishments would have been 1,464. The October 2018 survey covered 491.

A sample has to be reweighted to stand in for the whole. Tabulation up to 2017 assumed 1 sampling rate nationwide, so Tokyo was not reweighted. MHLW's fact sheet says amounts such as regular pay for 2004 to 2017 came out low. As far as it could confirm, the number of establishments surveyed had also run about 10% below the published number since 1996.

From January 2018 the tabulation reweighted Tokyo. It was not announced. In December 2018 the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC) pointed out a break between 2017 and 2018. The fact sheet adds that some staff knew before MIC raised it, and that this was not shared across the organisation.

In June 2018 MHLW had told Kanagawa, Aichi and Osaka it planned to sample their 500+ establishments from 2019. The fact sheet records that this has been withdrawn.

## Who lied

A Special Inspection Committee reported on 22 January 2019. It found the gap between the approved plan and the actual method was considered to violate Articles 9 and 11 of the Statistics Act. It did not find an intentional act of making a fundamental statistic untrue under Article 60, the criminal provision, because the 2018 reweighting was meant to improve accuracy.

Its supplementary report of 27 February went further. At a 2015 study group the division explained that the survey was a full count. In its 2016 application to change the survey plan it wrote the same. The committee found these were false statements, made in public settings, on the judgment of the division head, 真実に反することを認識しながら (knowing it was untrue). It did not find organised concealment. It defined concealment as knowingly and intentionally hiding a violation, and found the staff involved had seen the problem as statistically harmless.

MHLW's discipline list named 22 officials. 6 were serving, and the most senior, the administrative vice-minister, was reprimanded. The other 16 had already left the ministry and could not be disciplined. Those judged to deserve a pay cut were asked to return the equivalent amount voluntarily.

## The fix

For 2012 onward, where the data still existed, MHLW published re-tabulated values. Re-tabulated regular pay differed from the published figures by 0.6% on average. For 2004 to 2011 the data no longer existed, so MHLW built estimates for benefit purposes: the 2004 published value plus 0.6%, grown at the published rates.

Unemployment and workers' compensation benefits are indexed to the survey. MHLW's estimate for employment insurance alone was about 1,400 yen per person, across about 19 million person-cases, about 28 billion yen in all, paid as top-ups.

The full count of Tokyo's 500+ establishments resumed with the June 2019 release.
