# Exhibit 015 brief: police-recorded crime, England and Wales

Every item below was read by the main session in the primary document (local extractions in this folder: hmic2014.txt, pasc760.txt, ar268.txt, osr2024.txt, ons2016.txt, and the ONS Figure 1 sheet). Use nothing that is not here. If a sentence needs a fact not in this brief, leave it out.

## Shape
1 country, 2 official crime counts. The Crime Survey for England and Wales (CSEW) asks households; police-recorded crime (PRC) counts what forces record. ONS itself says PRC is "generally, not a reliable measure of trends in crime". In 2014, 3 institutions acted: the statistics regulator removed PRC's National Statistics designation (January), a Commons committee reported (April), the inspectorate audited every force (November) and found 19% of reported crimes unrecorded. The designation has not come back: in May 2024 the regulator still listed PRC as official statistics, not accredited. Twist available: by then the survey had lost its accreditation too, over response rates.

### S4. Office for Statistics Regulation, "The quality of police recorded crime statistics for England and Wales", published 16 May 2024
- "The police recorded crime statistics for England and Wales are published as official statistics, not accredited official statistics. We removed their National Statistics accreditation in 2014 following an assessment which found evidence that the quality and consistency of the underlying data may not be reliable."
- "This review is the first step towards the statistics being considered for reaccreditation."
- "Currently, the CSEW statistics are published as official statistics, not accredited official statistics, due to challenges with response rates that affect the quality of the estimates."
- HMICFRS inspections estimate recording accuracy improved "from 80.5% (± 2.0%) of all crimes (excluding fraud) in 2014 to 92.4% (± 0.3%) at the end of the 2021 to 2022 inspection programme, a statistically significant change".
- URL: https://osr.statisticsauthority.gov.uk/publication/the-quality-of-police-recorded-crime-statistics-for-england-and-wales/
- Note: 80.5% (OSR, ± 2.0) and 81% (HMIC footnote 7) are the same audit, rounded differently. 19% is HMIC's own headline; use it.

### S5. ONS, "Crime in England and Wales: year ending March 2016" (bulletin)
- "For the population and offence types it covers, the CSEW provides the better measure of trends on a consistent basis over time."
- "Police recorded crime is, generally, not a reliable measure of trends in crime, since it is prone to changes in recording practices and police activity (for example, a proactive operation on weapon offences) as well as changing behaviour in public reporting of crime."
- Police recorded violence against the person "increased by 27% in the latest year, largely as a result of improvements in crime recording processes" and an expanded sub-category.
- URL: https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/crimeandjustice/bulletins/crimeinenglandandwales/yearendingmar2016
