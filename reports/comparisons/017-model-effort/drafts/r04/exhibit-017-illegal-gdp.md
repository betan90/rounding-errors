## The incident

In 2014 the Office for National Statistics added illegal drugs and prostitution to UK GDP. The rule was not new. ESA95, section 1.13, already counted "illegal transactions to which all units involved consent". An ONS footnote: "There are no changes in this area between ESA95 and ESA10." What changed was an audit. GNI "is used in the calculation of a Member State's contribution to the EU budget", and a 2012 EU audit set areas Member States "have to address by 2014".

The ONS article, by Joshua Abramsky and Steve Drew, published the method. Annual prostitution volume is "P*C*52". The P came from 2004: the charity Eaves estimated about 7,000 off-street prostitutes in London, "by calling numbers advertised in various media and asking how many prostitutes were in each place". The Metropolitan Police counted 115 on the street at any one time. Scaled up by UK population, that "gives c. 58,000 prostitutes in the whole country."

Clients per week followed "a Netherlands assumption with some research support": ONS calculated estimates for 20, 25 and 30. The price, £55 in 2004, came from Punternet. Footnote 12, for Punternet, is a Wikipedia page.

"As there is no time series", ONS moved the 2004 figures through time with the 16+ male population and the CPI. ONS called this "a weak assumption".

## Who lied

Nobody. ONS labelled its own assumptions. On drug use per user: "This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it." On cannabis being half imported, half home-grown: "This is an arbitrary assumption." On government spending: reasonable "because government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs." "Probably false" appears 3 times in the article.

Peter Patterson, ONS Deputy Chief Economic Adviser, wrote on a gov.uk blog that "ONS desk research (please note the italics for emphasis!)" found these activities contribute "up to £10 billion a year to the level of GDP." He called inclusion "legally mandated".

## The bit continues

An ONS FOI reply gave prostitution for 2010 as "£5.6bn (current prices), which is 0.4% of GDP." In its Blue Book 2016 revisions, ONS reduced the estimates "by a factor to reflect the holiday taken by prostitutes. Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

Today's series, MNC8, gives 2010 as £4,370m. ONS does not say what accounts for the difference. This site's arithmetic, not ONS's: 4,370 is 78% of 5,600, and 40 weeks is 77% of 52.

The series runs from 1985, 29 years before the first estimate. It went from £5,993m in 2019 to £4,130m in 2020 and £6,142m in 2022. This site found no ONS explanation for the fall. The latest figure, for 2024, is £6,431m. The last release was 31 October 2025.
