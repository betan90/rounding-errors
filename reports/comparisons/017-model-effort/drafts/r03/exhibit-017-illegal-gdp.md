## The incident

The UK counts illegal drugs and prostitution in GDP. The ONS article explaining why starts with the EU budget: gross national income "is used in the calculation of a Member State's contribution to the EU budget", and a 2012 audit listed improvements Member States had to address by 2014. The rule is older. ESA95 states that "illegal transactions to which all units involved consent are included within the production boundary". ONS adds: "There are no changes in this area between ESA95 and ESA10."

In May 2014 Joshua Abramsky and Steve Drew published the method. It lists its own assumptions.

"We assume that the purity-adjusted amount of drugs consumed by the average user remains constant over time. This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it." "We assume that half of cannabis sold in the UK is imported and half home-grown. This is an arbitrary assumption." "The government final consumption expenditure assumption is reasonable because government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs." "Probably false" appears 3 times.

Prostitution has no time series. The charity Eaves estimated approx 7000 off-street prostitutes in London in 2004 by calling advertised numbers. The Metropolitan Police gave 115 on the street. Scaled to the UK: c. 58,000. The price, £55 in 2004, comes from Punternet. Footnote 12 for Punternet is a Wikipedia address.

The count is then moved through time with the male population, which ONS calls "a weak assumption".

## Who lied

Nobody. Each assumption is on the page, labelled. Peter Patterson, ONS Deputy Chief Economic Adviser, wrote on the Civil Service Quarterly blog that "ONS desk research (please note the italics for emphasis!)" produced a contribution of "up to £10 billion a year" to GDP. He wrote that "Comparability between nations dictates that all countries include it in their GDP figures."

An ONS FOI response gives N2, drugs and illegal prostitution, as £9,097m for 2010, 0.58% of GDP. It gives prostitution in household consumption as £5.6bn.

## The bit continues

The Blue Book 2016 revisions reduced the prostitution estimate "to reflect the holiday taken by prostitutes". ONS wrote: "Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

ONS series MNC8 now gives prostitution in 2010 as £4,370m. The FOI reply gave £5.6bn. Those are 2 published figures. By this site's arithmetic, 4,370 divided by 5,600 is 0.78, and 40 working weeks of 52 is 0.77. ONS does not say this is the whole difference.

MNC8 runs from 1985, although the first estimate was published in 2014. It rises from £1,078m in 1985 to £5,993m in 2019, then reads £4,130m in 2020 and £6,142m in 2022. The latest release is 31 October 2025.
