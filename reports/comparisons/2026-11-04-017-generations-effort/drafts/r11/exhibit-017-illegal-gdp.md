## The incident

Gross national income "is used in the calculation of a Member State's contribution to the EU budget". ONS records that in 2012, "following a comprehensive audit of the methods used across EU countries, a number of areas for improvement were identified which Member States have to address by 2014." Illegal activity was among them. ESA95 section 1.13 already placed illegal transactions "to which all units involved consent" inside the production boundary, and ONS notes "There are no changes in this area between ESA95 and ESA10."

In May 2014 ONS published the method, written by Joshua Abramsky and Steve Drew. Annual prostitution volume is "P*C*52". P came from a 2004 Eaves estimate of "approx 7000 off-street prostitutes in London in that year; this was done by calling numbers advertised in various media and asking how many prostitutes were in each place", plus a Metropolitan Police figure of "115 prostitutes on the street at any one time in London". "Adding these and scaling up by UK population ... gives c. 58,000 prostitutes in the whole country." C followed "a Netherlands assumption with some research support": 20, 25 and 30. The price, "£55 in 2004", came from Punternet, varied by the CPI for personal services. Footnote 12, for Punternet, is a Wikipedia URL.

The article labels its own inputs. The number of prostitutes is assumed to track the 16+ male population: "This is a weak assumption ... It is necessary because we have no time series data for the number of prostitutes." Splitting cannabis supply half imported, half home-grown: "This is an arbitrary assumption." Holding per-user drug consumption constant: "This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it." Government spending on drugs is set at 0, because "government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs."

## Who lied

Nobody. ONS published the assumptions with their weaknesses marked, and said the article "does not include numerical impacts".

The impact arrived in October 2014, on a gov.uk blog, from ONS's then Deputy Chief Economic Adviser Peter Patterson: "ONS desk research (please note the italics for emphasis!) into the numbers of prostitutes and the price of drugs yielded the result that spending on these illegal activities in the UK contributes up to £10 billion a year to the level of GDP."

## The bit continues

Blue Book 2016 brought a refinement: "allowance was made to reduce the estimates made for prostitution in household final consumption expenditure by a factor to reflect the holiday taken by prostitutes. Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

An ONS freedom of information response put prostitution in 2010 at "£5.6bn (current prices), which is 0.4% of GDP". Today's series puts 2010 at £4,370m.

Series MNC8 runs from 1985 to 2024 and was last released on 31 October 2025. It starts at £1,078m and ends at £6,431m.
