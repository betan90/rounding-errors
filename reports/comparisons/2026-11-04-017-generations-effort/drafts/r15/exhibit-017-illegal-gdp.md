## The incident

Gross national income "is used in the calculation of a Member State's contribution to the EU budget". A 2012 audit of "the methods used across EU countries" identified "a number of areas for improvement ... which Member States have to address by 2014". The rule was older: ESA95 put illegal transactions "to which all units involved consent" inside the production boundary.

In May 2014, ONS published its method for illegal drugs and prostitution, written by Joshua Abramsky and Steve Drew. The summary warns of "significant limitations in the availability of data", which "requires a number of assumptions", and says this "applies especially to the estimates of prostitution activity". The article "does not include numerical impacts".

Annual volume is "P*C*52": prostitutes, clients per week, weeks. For prostitutes, ONS took the charity Eaves's 2004 estimate of "approx 7000 off-street prostitutes in London", made "by calling numbers advertised in various media and asking how many prostitutes were in each place", plus a Metropolitan Police figure of "115 prostitutes on the street at any one time in London". "Adding these and scaling up by UK population ... gives c. 58,000 prostitutes in the whole country."

Clients per week: "Following a Netherlands assumption with some research support ... we calculate estimates based on 20, 25 and 30." Price: "£55 in 2004", from "an estimate from Punternet supported by a 2004 journal article", moved by the CPI for personal services. The footnote for Punternet is a Wikipedia URL.

## Who lied

Nobody. ONS labelled its own inputs. The number of prostitutes was assumed to have "the same pattern through time as the 16+ male population". ONS: "This is a weak assumption ... It is necessary because we have no time series data for the number of prostitutes."

The drugs table is plainer. On consumption per user: "This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it." On cannabis being half imported, half home-grown: "This is an arbitrary assumption." On government spending: "The government final consumption expenditure assumption is reasonable because government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs." In the article, "probably false" appears 3 times and "arbitrary" twice.

ONS's Deputy Chief Economic Adviser, Peter Patterson, described the exercise on a government blog in October 2014: "ONS desk research (please note the italics for emphasis!) into the numbers of prostitutes and the price of drugs yielded the result that spending on these illegal activities in the UK contributes up to £10 billion a year to the level of GDP."

## The bit continues

Blue Book 2016 refined the estimates. "Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

An ONS freedom of information response puts 2010 prostitution at "£5.6bn (current prices), which is 0.4% of GDP". The published series now gives 2010 as £4,370m.

The series runs back to 1985. ONS's first estimate appeared in 2014. The latest release is dated 31 October 2025.
