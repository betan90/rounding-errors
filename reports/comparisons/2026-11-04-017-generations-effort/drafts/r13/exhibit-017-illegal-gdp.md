## The incident

The European System of National Accounts has required member states to include illegal transactions in GDP since ESA95. The rule, as ONS put it in 2014: "illegal transactions to which all units involved consent are included within the production boundary." In the UK, that meant drugs and prostitution.

ONS published its method in May 2014. The prostitution estimate starts with a 2004 count: the charity Eaves "estimated that there were approx 7000 off-street prostitutes in London in that year; this was done by calling numbers advertised in various media and asking how many prostitutes were in each place." The Metropolitan Police counted 115 on the street. ONS scaled the total by UK population to get roughly 58,000.

For price, the method cites "an estimate from Punternet supported by a 2004 journal article. This is £55 in 2004." Footnote 12 for Punternet reads: "http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punternet".

The assumptions are labeled. Number 1: "the number of prostitutes has the same pattern through time as the 16+ male population. This is a weak assumption ... It is necessary because we have no time series data for the number of prostitutes." Number 3b: "There is no employment in prostitution; all prostitutes and pimps are self-employed. This is a controversial assumption."

For drugs, the table of assumptions is more direct. "We assume that the purity-adjusted amount of drugs consumed by the average user remains constant over time. This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it." On the import split for cannabis: "This is an arbitrary assumption." On government consumption: "government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs."

## Who lied

Nobody. ONS published every assumption and flagged each weakness. The method article states that the estimates have "significant limitations in the availability of data" and are "partly based" on assumptions, "especially the estimates of prostitution activity."

## The bit continues

In 2016 ONS revised the estimates downward. The Blue Book 2016 revisions article: "allowance was made to reduce the estimates made for prostitution in household final consumption expenditure by a factor to reflect the holiday taken by prostitutes. Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

A 2015 FOI response put 2010 prostitution at £5.6bn. The current MNC8 series puts the same year at £4,370m. The series continues: the 2024 figure is £6,431m, released 31 October 2025.
