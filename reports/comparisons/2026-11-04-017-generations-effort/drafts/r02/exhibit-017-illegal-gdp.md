## The incident

Gross national income "is used in the calculation of a Member State's contribution to the EU budget". On 29 May 2014 ONS published its article on including illegal drugs and prostitution in the UK national accounts.

The rule is older. ESA95 section 1.13 "states that illegal transactions to which all units involved consent are included within the production boundary". A footnote adds: "There are no changes in this area between ESA95 and ESA10."

The article says its estimates are "partly based" on assumptions, "especially" for prostitution. Eaves, a charity, estimated in 2004 "approx 7000 off-street prostitutes in London" by "calling numbers advertised in various media and asking how many prostitutes were in each place". The Metropolitan Police gave "115 prostitutes on the street at any one time in London". Adding these and scaling by UK population gave "c. 58,000".

Clients per week followed "a Netherlands assumption": ONS calculated estimates for 20, 25 and 30. The 2004 price, £55, came from "an estimate from Punternet". Footnote 12 for Punternet is a Wikipedia address.

For drugs, Table 3 records: "We assume that half of cannabis sold in the UK is imported and half home-grown. This is an arbitrary assumption." The government final consumption expenditure assumption is "reasonable because government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs."

## Who lied

Nobody. ONS published every assumption and labelled several itself: "probably false" appears 3 times in the article, "arbitrary" 2 times.

Peter Patterson, ONS Deputy Chief Economic Adviser, wrote in a Civil Service Quarterly blog on 15 October 2014 that spending on these illegal activities "contributes up to £10 billion a year to the level of GDP", a result of "ONS desk research (please note the italics for emphasis!)".

## The bit continues

Blue Book 2016 added a refinement: the prostitution estimates were reduced "by a factor to reflect the holiday taken by prostitutes. Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

An ONS FOI response gave prostitution in 2010 as "£5.6bn (current prices), which is 0.4% of GDP". The current series, MNC8, released 31 October 2025, gives £4,370m for 2010. These are 2 published figures. This site's arithmetic: 4,370 divided by 5,600 is 0.78, and 40 of 52 weeks is 0.77. ONS does not state that the holiday is the whole difference.

MNC8 runs from 1985 to 2024, £1,078m to £6,431m. ONS first published an estimate in 2014. Under the 2014 method "there is no time series", so a 2004 point estimate is varied by the male population, "a weak assumption", and the CPI for personal services. The series is modelled, not observed. In 2019 it was £5,993m; in 2020, £4,130m, down 31%; in 2022, £6,142m.
