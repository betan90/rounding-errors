## The incident

A Member State's contribution to the EU budget is calculated from gross national income (GNI). ESA95, section 1.13, includes in the production boundary "illegal transactions to which all units involved consent". ONS notes no change in this area between ESA95 and ESA 2010. ONS's Blue Book 2016 article describes its illegal-activities work as part of addressing a GNI ESA 1995 reservation.

On 29 May 2014 ONS published the method. Its authors were Joshua Abramsky and Steve Drew. The estimates rest on "a number of assumptions", and "this applies especially to the estimates of prostitution activity."

The headcount: Eaves, a charity, estimated approx 7000 off-street prostitutes in London in 2004 by calling advertised numbers and asking how many were in each place. The Metropolitan Police gave 115 on the street at any one time. Scaled up by UK population, c. 58,000. There is no time series, so ONS varies the 2004 point estimate by the 16+ male population. It calls this "a weak assumption".

The price is £55 in 2004, from Punternet, varied by the CPI for personal services. Footnote 12 gives the source as http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punternet. Clients per week follow "a Netherlands assumption": 20, 25 and 30 were tested.

## Who lied

Nobody. ONS published every assumption and said what it thought of it.

On drug use per user: "This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it." On cannabis: "We assume that half of cannabis sold in the UK is imported and half home-grown. This is an arbitrary assumption." On other drugs: "While this is probably false, we do not have any evidence to the contrary." On government spending: "reasonable because government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs." The article uses "probably false" 3 times and "arbitrary" 2 times.

Peter Patterson, ONS Deputy Chief Economic Adviser, wrote on the Civil Service Quarterly blog in October 2014 that the spending contributes "up to £10 billion a year to the level of GDP". On inclusion: "Comparability between nations dictates that all countries include it in their GDP figures."

## The bit continues

The Blue Book 2016 article reduced the prostitution estimates "to reflect the holiday taken by prostitutes". "Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

An ONS FOI response puts prostitution in 2010 at £5.6bn. Series MNC8, released 31 October 2025, puts 2010 at £4,370m. These are 2 published figures. By this site's arithmetic, 4,370 over 5,600 is 0.78, and 40 working weeks over 52 is 0.77. ONS does not say that the holiday is the whole difference.

The series starts in 1985, 29 years before ONS's first estimate. It reads £5,993m in 2019, £4,130m in 2020, down 31%, and £6,142m in 2022. No ONS explanation of the 2020 figure was found.
