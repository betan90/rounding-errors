## The incident

A 2012 EU audit of national accounting methods identified areas Member States "have to address by 2014". GNI "is used in the calculation of a Member State's contribution to the EU budget". ESA95, section 1.13, already required that "illegal transactions to which all units involved consent are included within the production boundary".

So in May 2014 ONS published the method for putting illegal drugs and prostitution into UK GDP. It contained no numbers. It contained the assumptions.

The volume of prostitution is "P*C*52". P came from a 2004 charity estimate of "approx 7000 off-street prostitutes in London in that year", made "by calling numbers advertised in various media and asking how many prostitutes were in each place", plus a Metropolitan Police figure of "115 prostitutes on the street at any one time in London". ONS: "Adding these and scaling up by UK population ... gives c. 58,000 prostitutes in the whole country."

C followed "a Netherlands assumption with some research support", and ONS "calculate estimates based on 20, 25 and 30". The price, £55 in 2004, came from Punternet "supported by a 2004 journal article". The footnote for Punternet is a Wikipedia page.

## Who lied

Nobody. ONS graded its own inputs in print.

On the time series: "This is a weak assumption ... It is necessary because we have no time series data for the number of prostitutes."

The drugs table is firmer. On consumption per user: "This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it." On cannabis: "We assume that half of cannabis sold in the UK is imported and half home-grown. This is an arbitrary assumption." On domestic production: "We assume that no drugs other than cannabis are produced in the UK. While this is probably false, we do not have any evidence to the contrary." On government spending: "The government final consumption expenditure assumption is reasonable because government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs."

## The bit continues

Blue Book 2016 reduced household spending on prostitution "by a factor to reflect the holiday taken by prostitutes": "Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

An ONS FOI reply gave 2010 prostitution as "£5.6bn (current prices), which is 0.4% of GDP", and illegal production overall as 0.58% of GDP. The same year in the series published today is 4,370.

The series was last released on 31 October 2025. It now runs to 2024.
