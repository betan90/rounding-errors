## The incident

UK GDP includes illegal drugs and prostitution. The European System of National Accounts 1995 (ESA95), section 1.13, "states that illegal transactions to which all units involved consent are included within the production boundary". ESA 2010 keeps the rule.

On 29 May 2014 ONS published its method in an article by Joshua Abramsky and Steve Drew. The article cites "significant limitations in the availability of data" and "a number of assumptions on which the estimates are, therefore, partly based. This applies especially to the estimates of prostitution activity."

ONS used 2 London figures for the number of prostitutes. In 2004 Eaves, a charity, "estimated that there were approx 7000 off-street prostitutes in London in that year; this was done by calling numbers advertised in various media and asking how many prostitutes were in each place". The Metropolitan Police figure was "115 prostitutes on the street at any one time in London". Adding these and scaling up by UK population "gives c. 58,000 prostitutes in the whole country."

Clients per week followed "a Netherlands assumption with some research support". The price, £55 in 2004, is "an estimate from Punternet supported by a 2004 journal article". Footnote 12, for Punternet, is its Wikipedia page.

Because "we have no time series data for the number of prostitutes", the article assumes the number "has the same pattern through time as the 16+ male population."

## Who lied

Nobody. The article labels its own assumptions. It calls the male population pattern "a weak assumption". For drugs, the purity-adjusted amount consumed by the average user is held constant over time: "This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it." Cannabis is assumed half imported and half home-grown: "This is an arbitrary assumption." "Probably false" appears 3 times in the article and "arbitrary" 2 times.

The article finds the government final consumption expenditure assumption "reasonable because government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs."

Peter Patterson, ONS Deputy Chief Economic Adviser, wrote on the Civil Service Quarterly blog in October 2014 that spending on illegal activities "contributes up to £10 billion a year to the level of GDP" and that "Comparability between nations dictates that all countries include it in their GDP figures."

## The bit continues

The series is still published. ONS series MNC8, modelled household spending on prostitution, has 40 annual values, 1985 to 2024, in the release of 31 October 2025. The values are £5,993m for 2019, £4,130m for 2020 (down 31%) and £6,142m for 2022.

The Blue Book 2016 revisions article lists a reduction in the prostitution estimates "to reflect the holiday taken by prostitutes. Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

An ONS Freedom of Information response gives prostitution spending for 2010 as "£5.6bn (current prices)". Series MNC8 gives £4,370m for 2010.
