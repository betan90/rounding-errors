## The incident

ONS's article of 29 May 2014, by Joshua Abramsky and Steve Drew, explains why illegal drugs and prostitution are in the national accounts: gross national income "is used in the calculation of a Member State's contribution to the EU budget".

The rule is in the European System of National Accounts 1995, section 1.13: "illegal transactions to which all units involved consent are included within the production boundary".

For prostitution, Eaves, a charity, in 2004 "estimated that there were approx 7000 off-street prostitutes in London in that year; this was done by calling numbers advertised in various media and asking how many prostitutes were in each place". Adding the Metropolitan Police's street count and "scaling up by UK population" gives "c. 58,000 prostitutes in the whole country".

Clients per week follow "a Netherlands assumption with some research support". The price is "an estimate from Punternet supported by a 2004 journal article. This is £55 in 2004." Footnote 12, for Punternet, reads "http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punternet".

## Who lied

Nobody. ONS published the assumptions and said what it thought of them.

Purity-adjusted consumption per user is held constant over time: "This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it." On cannabis: "We assume that half of cannabis sold in the UK is imported and half home-grown. This is an arbitrary assumption." On government spending on drugs: "The government final consumption expenditure assumption is reasonable because government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs."

For prostitution, Assumption 1 is that "the number of prostitutes has the same pattern through time as the 16+ male population. This is a weak assumption", needed "because we have no time series data for the number of prostitutes".

Peter Patterson, ONS Deputy Chief Economic Adviser, blogged: "ONS desk research (please note the italics for emphasis!) into the numbers of prostitutes and the price of drugs yielded the result that spending on these illegal activities in the UK contributes up to £10 billion a year to the level of GDP."

## The bit continues

ONS series MNC8, household spending on prostitution at current prices, runs from 1985 to 2024 (release of 31 October 2025). It is modelled, not observed. It fell 31% in 2020, from £5,993m to £4,130m, and was £6,142m in 2022.

A refinement listed in the Blue Book 2016 revisions article reduced the prostitution estimates "by a factor to reflect the holiday taken by prostitutes": "Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

An ONS FOI response gives the 2010 value for prostitution as "£5.6bn (current prices), which is 0.4% of GDP". MNC8 gives 2010 as £4,370m. That is 2 published figures for 1 year. This site's arithmetic, not ONS's: 4,370 divided by 5,600 is 0.78, and 40 of 52 weeks is 0.77. ONS does not state that the holiday factor is the whole difference.
