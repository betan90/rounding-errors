## The incident

The European System of National Accounts 1995 (ESA95) required member states to include illegal activities in GDP. Section 1.13: "illegal transactions to which all units involved consent are included within the production boundary." An ONS footnote adds: "There are no changes in this area between ESA95 and ESA10."

In 2014 ONS published its method. The article, by Joshua Abramsky and Steve Drew, notes "significant limitations in the availability of data" and warns that the estimates "are, therefore, partly based" on assumptions. "This applies especially to the estimates of prostitution activity."

The number of prostitutes: in 2004, a charity called brothels advertised in London and counted; the Met Police estimated 115 on the street. Adding these and scaling by UK population gave c. 58,000. ONS: "there is no time series" for that count. It varies the 2004 figure by the male population.

The price: £55 in 2004, from a site called Punternet, varied by the CPI. Footnote 12 points to Wikipedia.

The assumptions table for drugs includes: "We assume that the purity-adjusted amount of drugs consumed by the average user remains constant over time. This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it." Also: "We assume that half of cannabis sold in the UK is imported and half home-grown. This is an arbitrary assumption." And: "The government final consumption expenditure assumption is reasonable because government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs."

"Probably false" appears 3 times. "Arbitrary" appears twice.

## Who lied

Nobody. ONS published every assumption.

Peter Patterson, the agency's Deputy Chief Economic Adviser, wrote on the Civil Service Quarterly blog: "ONS desk research (please note the italics for emphasis!) into the numbers of prostitutes and the price of drugs yielded the result that spending on these illegal activities in the UK contributes up to £10 billion a year to the level of GDP."

## The bit continues

In Blue Book 2016 ONS refined the series. "Allowance was made to reduce the estimates made for prostitution in household final consumption expenditure by a factor to reflect the holiday taken by prostitutes. Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

The 2010 figure in a 2015 FOI response was £5.6 billion. The 2010 figure in the current series is £4,370 million. 2 published figures, from the same office, for the same year.

The series runs from 1985 to 2024. ONS has no data for 1985. It has a 2004 point estimate, and assumptions.
