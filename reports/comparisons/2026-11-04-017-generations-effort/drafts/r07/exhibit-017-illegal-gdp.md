## The incident

Since the Blue Book 2014 improvements, UK GDP has measured spending on illegal drugs and prostitution. ONS's 2014 article on the change, by Joshua Abramsky and Steve Drew, cites ESA95 section 1.13, which puts "illegal transactions to which all units involved consent" inside the production boundary. ONS says nothing in this area changed between ESA95 and ESA 2010.

The reason was the EU budget. Gross national income "is used in the calculation of a Member State's contribution to the EU budget", and a 2012 audit set improvements that Member States "have to address by 2014".

Prostitution is estimated from counts. A 2004 charity estimate gave "approx 7000 off-street prostitutes in London", and a Metropolitan Police count gave "115 prostitutes on the street at any one time in London". Scaled up, the sum "gives c. 58,000 prostitutes in the whole country". The price, £55 in 2004, is an estimate from Punternet. Its footnote links to Wikipedia.

ONS assumes the amount consumed by the average user "remains constant over time. This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it." It assumes half of UK cannabis is imported: "This is an arbitrary assumption." It excludes government spending on drugs because "government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs."

## Who lied

Nobody. ONS published each assumption beside its weakness. The article says the estimates "are, therefore, partly based" on assumptions, and that this "applies especially to the estimates of prostitution activity."

In 2014, Peter Patterson, ONS's deputy chief economic adviser, wrote on the government's Civil Service Quarterly blog that the overall effect "has been to raise the level of GDP by around £50 billion a year".

## The bit continues

The 2016 Blue Book revisions added a holiday deduction. ONS made an allowance "to reduce the estimates made for prostitution" by a factor reflecting "the holiday taken by prostitutes". The factor came from the Netherlands: "Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year."

A FOI reply gave 2010 prostitution spending as "£5.6bn (current prices), which is 0.4% of GDP". The current MNC8 series gives £4,370m for the same year. This site's arithmetic: 4,370 is 78% of 5,600, and 40 of 52 weeks is 77%. ONS does not say the holiday deduction explains the gap.

The chart plots the MNC8 series from 1985 to 2024. It is modelled, not observed. ONS's method says "there is no time series", so the 2004 estimate is varied by the male population. The series falls to 4,130 in 2020 from 5,993 in 2019. The sources cited here do not explain the fall. It reached 6,431 in 2024.
