## The incident

Gross National Income (GNI) "is used in the calculation of a Member State's contribution to the EU budget". After a 2012 audit, Member States had to address areas for improvement by 2014. One was illegal activity. The European System of Accounts 1995 (ESA95) already placed "illegal transactions to which all units involved consent" within the production boundary. ONS: "There are no changes in this area between ESA95 and ESA10."

On 29 May 2014 ONS published, by Joshua Abramsky and Steve Drew, the method for adding illegal drugs and prostitution to the UK accounts. It said: "This applies especially to the estimates of prostitution activity."

Prostitution is prostitutes, times clients, times 52 weeks. Eaves, a charity, estimated 7,000 off-street prostitutes in London in 2004 by "calling numbers advertised in various media and asking how many prostitutes were in each place". The Metropolitan Police reported "115 prostitutes on the street at any one time in London". Added and scaled by UK population: c. 58,000 for the country. Clients per week came "Following a Netherlands assumption with some research support", at 20, 25 and 30. The price, £55 in 2004, came from Punternet. Footnote 12 is http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punternet.

"As there is no time series, we vary the point estimates in time using the male population." ONS called that "a weak assumption", "necessary because we have no time series data for the number of prostitutes".

## Who lied

Nobody. ONS published every assumption, with its own rating.

"This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it." "We assume that half of cannabis sold in the UK is imported and half home-grown. This is an arbitrary assumption." "The government final consumption expenditure assumption is reasonable because government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs." "There is no employment in prostitution; all prostitutes and pimps are self-employed. This is a controversial assumption".

"probably false" appears 3 times in the article. "arbitrary" appears 2.

Peter Patterson, ONS Deputy Chief Economic Adviser, wrote on the Civil Service Quarterly blog in October 2014: "ONS desk research (please note the italics for emphasis!) into the numbers of prostitutes and the price of drugs yielded the result that spending on these illegal activities in the UK contributes up to £10 billion a year to the level of GDP."

## The bit continues

Blue Book 2016 reduced the prostitution estimates "by a factor to reflect the holiday taken by prostitutes". Its reason: "Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

ONS has published 2 figures for 2010 prostitution spending. Its FOI response gives £5.6bn. Series MNC8 now gives £4,370m. 4,370 divided by 5,600 is 0.78; 40 working weeks of 52 is 0.77. That arithmetic is this site's. ONS does not say the holiday is the whole difference.

MNC8 runs from 1985, 29 years before the first estimate. It reads £5,993m in 2019, £4,130m in 2020, down 31%, and £6,142m in 2022. This site states the numbers only.

The last release was 31 October 2025.
