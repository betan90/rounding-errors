## The incident

ONS's 2014 article notes that GNI "is used in the calculation of a Member State's contribution to the EU budget". In 2012 an EU audit identified areas for improvement "which Member States have to address by 2014".

The rule was old. ESA95 "section 1.13 - states that illegal transactions to which all units involved consent are included within the production boundary", and a footnote adds: "There are no changes in this area between ESA95 and ESA10."

So ONS priced prostitution. Annual volume is "P*C*52". On the first term: "As there is no time series, we vary the point estimates in time using the male population."

The charity Eaves in 2004 "estimated that there were approx 7000 off-street prostitutes in London in that year; this was done by calling numbers advertised in various media and asking how many prostitutes were in each place". The Metropolitan Police put "115 prostitutes on the street at any one time in London". "Adding these and scaling up by UK population ... gives c. 58,000 prostitutes in the whole country."

Clients per week: "Following a Netherlands assumption with some research support ... we calculate estimates based on 20, 25 and 30." Price: "an estimate from Punternet supported by a 2004 journal article. This is £55 in 2004", varied by the CPI for personal services. The footnote for Punternet is a Wikipedia URL.

## Who lied

Nobody. Abramsky and Drew numbered and rated the assumptions. Assumption 1 is that the number of prostitutes tracks the 16+ male population: "This is a weak assumption ... It is necessary because we have no time series data for the number of prostitutes." Assumption 3b: "There is no employment in prostitution; all prostitutes and pimps are self-employed. This is a controversial assumption".

Same for drugs. "We assume that half of cannabis sold in the UK is imported and half home-grown. This is an arbitrary assumption." And: "The government final consumption expenditure assumption is reasonable because government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs." On consumption per user: "This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it."

"probably false" appears 3 times in the article.

## The bit continues

Blue Book 2016 brought a refinement: "allowance was made to reduce the estimates made for prostitution in household final consumption expenditure by a factor to reflect the holiday taken by prostitutes. Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

An ONS freedom of information reply put 2010 prostitution at "£5.6bn (current prices), which is 0.4% of GDP". Series MNC8 puts 2010 at £4,370m.

Patterson, ONS Deputy Chief Economic Adviser, wrote in 2014 that "ONS desk research (please note the italics for emphasis!) into the numbers of prostitutes and the price of drugs yielded the result that spending on these illegal activities in the UK contributes up to £10 billion a year to the level of GDP." The series was last released on 31 October 2025.
