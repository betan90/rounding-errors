## The incident

National accounts in the EU must include illegal activity. ESA95 section 1.13 says consensual illegal transactions "are included within the production boundary", and ONS notes this is unchanged in its successor: "There are no changes in this area between ESA95 and ESA 2010." The reason to care: GNI "is used in the calculation of a Member State's contribution to the EU budget", and a 2012 audit identified areas for Member States to address by 2014.

So in 2014 ONS added drugs and prostitution to UK GDP. Peter Patterson, then Deputy Chief Economic Adviser, wrote on a gov.uk blog that "ONS desk research (please note the italics for emphasis!) into the numbers of prostitutes and the price of drugs yielded the result that spending on these illegal activities in the UK contributes up to £10 billion a year to the level of GDP."

The method is the exhibit. There is no survey, so ONS built the number from assumptions. Prostitution volume is "P*C*52". For the headcount, a charity's 2004 London estimate of "approx 7000 off-street prostitutes" and a Metropolitan Police count of "115 prostitutes on the street at any one time" were added and "scaling up by UK population ... gives c. 58,000 prostitutes in the whole country." Clients per week came from "a Netherlands assumption". The price came from Punternet, footnoted to a Wikipedia page.

"As there is no time series, we vary the point estimates in time using the male population." ONS calls that "a weak assumption".

## Who lied

Nobody. ONS flagged every weakness itself. The drugs table reads: "This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it." On cannabis: "We assume that half of cannabis sold in the UK is imported and half home-grown. This is an arbitrary assumption." On public spending: "The government final consumption expenditure assumption is reasonable because government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs." The phrase "probably false" appears 3 times.

## The bit continues

The series is still published. The latest release is 31 October 2025.

In Blue Book 2016 ONS refined the estimate, reducing it "by a factor to reflect the holiday taken by prostitutes. Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

The figures moved. A November 2015 FOI reply gives 2010 prostitution as "£5.6bn (current prices), which is 0.4% of GDP". Today's MNC8 series gives 2010 as £4,370m. The ratio of the 2 figures is 0.78; 40 of 52 weeks is 0.77. ONS does not say the holiday is the whole difference. That arithmetic is this site's, not ONS's.

The modelled series runs from 1985. It reads £4,130m in 2020 and £6,431m in 2024.
