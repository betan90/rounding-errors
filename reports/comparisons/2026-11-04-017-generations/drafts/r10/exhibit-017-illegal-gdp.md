## The incident

On 29 May 2014 ONS published "Changes to National Accounts: Inclusion of Illegal Drugs and Prostitution in the UK National Accounts", by Joshua Abramsky and Steve Drew. The rule was old: ESA95 already counted illegal transactions "to which all units involved consent", and the article notes "no changes in this area between ESA95 and ESA10." The audit was new. GNI feeds into "a Member State's contribution to the EU budget", and a 2012 audit across EU countries found areas "which Member States have to address by 2014."

The annual volume is "P\*C\*52". For the number of prostitutes, the charity Eaves estimated about 7,000 off-street in London in 2004, "by calling numbers advertised in various media and asking how many prostitutes were in each place". The Metropolitan Police figure was 115 on the street "at any one time". Scaled up by UK population: "c. 58,000 prostitutes in the whole country." Clients per week followed "a Netherlands assumption with some research support": ONS calculated estimates on 20, 25 and 30. The price, £55 in 2004, is "an estimate from Punternet supported by a 2004 journal article". The footnote for Punternet is a Wikipedia page.

## Who lied

Nobody. ONS graded its assumptions in print.

Cannabis: "We assume that half of cannabis sold in the UK is imported and half home-grown. This is an arbitrary assumption."

Drug consumption per user, held constant over time: "This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it."

Prostitutes tracking the 16+ male population: "This is a weak assumption".

Government: "The government final consumption expenditure assumption is reasonable because government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs."

The article "does not include numerical impacts". Peter Patterson, ONS Deputy Chief Economic Adviser, blogged a figure in October 2014: "ONS desk research (please note the italics for emphasis!) into the numbers of prostitutes and the price of drugs" put these activities at "up to £10 billion a year".

## The bit continues

ONS publishes the result as series MNC8: household spending on prostitution, £m, 1985 to 2024, last released 31 October 2025. The 2014 method says "there is no time series"; it varies the point estimates by the male population, and the price by the CPI for personal services. The chart plots the assumptions.

Blue Book 2016 reduced the estimates "to reflect the holiday taken by prostitutes": "Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

An ONS freedom of information reply put 2010 prostitution spending at £5.6bn. MNC8 now puts it at £4,370m. On this site's arithmetic, not ONS's, 4,370 is 78% of 5,600, and 40 weeks are 77% of 52. ONS does not say the holiday is the whole difference.
