## The incident

ONS: gross national income "is used in the calculation of a Member State's contribution to the EU budget". In 2012, "following a comprehensive audit of the methods used across EU countries, a number of areas for improvement were identified which Member States have to address by 2014." Illegal activity was 1 of them. ESA95 section 1.13 already put illegal transactions to which all units consent inside the production boundary, and ONS notes "There are no changes in this area between ESA95 and ESA10."

In May 2014 Joshua Abramsky and Steve Drew published the method. Its executive summary flagged "significant limitations in the availability of data" requiring "a number of assumptions", and warned that this "applies especially to the estimates of prostitution activity."

The prostitution volume is prostitutes times clients times 52. The count starts from a 2004 charity estimate of "approx 7000 off-street prostitutes in London", reached "by calling numbers advertised in various media and asking how many prostitutes were in each place", plus a Metropolitan Police figure of "115 prostitutes on the street at any one time in London". "Adding these and scaling up by UK population ... gives c. 58,000 prostitutes in the whole country." Clients per week follow "a Netherlands assumption with some research support". The price is £55 in 2004, "an estimate from Punternet supported by a 2004 journal article", moved by the CPI for personal services. ONS's footnote for Punternet is a Wikipedia URL.

"As there is no time series, we vary the point estimates in time using the male population." ONS's grade: "This is a weak assumption".

## Who lied

Nobody. The assumptions are published, numbered and graded by their authors. On drug consumption per user: "This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it." On supply: "We assume that half of cannabis sold in the UK is imported and half home-grown. This is an arbitrary assumption." ONS says "probably false" 3 times and "arbitrary" twice. Treating every prostitute and pimp as self-employed is "a controversial assumption". 1 assumption is defended as sound: "The government final consumption expenditure assumption is reasonable because government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs."

Peter Patterson, then ONS Deputy Chief Economic Adviser, wrote in 2014 that "ONS desk research (please note the italics for emphasis!) into the numbers of prostitutes and the price of drugs" yielded "up to £10 billion a year to the level of GDP".

## The bit continues

Blue Book 2016 carried a refinement: "allowance was made to reduce the estimates made for prostitution in household final consumption expenditure by a factor to reflect the holiday taken by prostitutes. Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

Series MNC8 was last released on 31 October 2025. It runs from £1,078m in 1985, 29 years before the first estimate was published, to £6,431m in 2024.
