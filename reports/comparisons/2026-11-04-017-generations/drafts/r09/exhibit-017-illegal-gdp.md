## The incident

The rule predates the estimate. The European System of National Accounts 1995 (ESA95), section 1.13, "states that illegal transactions to which all units involved consent are included within the production boundary". ONS adds: "There are no changes in this area between ESA95 and ESA10."

The UK's gross national income "is used in the calculation of a Member State's contribution to the EU budget". A 2012 audit of EU countries' methods found areas for improvement to be addressed "by 2014". On 29 May 2014 ONS published its method for adding illegal drugs and prostitution to GDP. The article "does not include numerical impacts". Its summary warns of "a number of assumptions on which the estimates are, therefore, partly based. This applies especially to the estimates of prostitution activity."

For London in 2004, the charity Eaves estimated about 7,000 off-street prostitutes, by "calling numbers advertised in various media and asking how many prostitutes were in each place". The Metropolitan Police added 115 on the street at any one time. Scaled up by UK population: "c. 58,000 prostitutes in the whole country." Clients per week came from "a Netherlands assumption with some research support": 20, 25 and 30. Price: £55 in 2004, from Punternet, "supported by a 2004 journal article". Footnote 12, for Punternet, is a Wikipedia URL.

"As there is no time series, we vary the point estimates in time using the male population". ONS on that: "This is a weak assumption".

## Who lied

Nobody. ONS printed every assumption beside its own assessment of it.

"We assume that half of cannabis sold in the UK is imported and half home-grown. This is an arbitrary assumption." On consumption per user: "This assumption is probably false, but we have no data to challenge it." On government spending: "government units are very unlikely to spend money on illegal drugs." On employment: "all prostitutes and pimps are self-employed. This is a controversial assumption".

In October 2014 Peter Patterson, ONS Deputy Chief Economic Adviser, blogged that "ONS desk research (please note the italics for emphasis!)" put these activities at "up to £10 billion a year to the level of GDP". Inclusion, he wrote, is "legally mandated through the European System of Accounts 2010".

## The bit continues

An ONS freedom of information reply put 2010's drugs and prostitution at £9,097m, 0.58% of GDP, and prostitution alone at £5.6bn.

In Blue Book 2016, ONS reduced the prostitution estimate "by a factor to reflect the holiday taken by prostitutes. Statistics Netherlands have estimated the total holidays to be 12 weeks per year and the same factor has been applied to the UK estimates."

Today's series, MNC8, gives prostitution in 2010 as £4,370m. By this site's arithmetic, not ONS's, that is 78% of £5.6bn; 40 weeks out of 52 is 77%. ONS does not say the holiday is the whole difference.

The series starts in 1985. It went from £5,993m in 2019 to £4,130m in 2020 and £6,431m in 2024. Its last release was 31 October 2025.
