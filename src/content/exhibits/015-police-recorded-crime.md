## The incident

England and Wales publish 2 official counts of crime. The Crime Survey for England and Wales (CSEW) asks households. Police-recorded crime counts what forces record. ONS says the survey "provides the better measure of trends" and that police-recorded crime "is, generally, not a reliable measure of trends in crime", being "prone to changes in recording practices and police activity".

In January 2014 the UK Statistics Authority cited "accumulating evidence that suggests the underlying data on crimes recorded by the police may not be reliable" and removed the National Statistics designation from statistics based on recorded crime data. It would stay off until ONS could show the data and the audit procedures "are sufficient".

In November 2014 HM Inspectorate of Constabulary (HMIC) reported on its audit of every force. It found 81% of reported crime being recorded. "Over 800,000 crimes reported to the police have gone unrecorded each year. This represents an under-recording of 19 percent." For violence against the person the rate was 33%; for sexual offences, 26%. Of 3,246 decisions to cancel a crime record, 664 were incorrect, including over 200 rapes.

96% of the records it reviewed were classified correctly.

## Who lied

Nobody. The record contains under-recording, and the institutions describing it.

The Commons Public Administration Select Committee reported in April 2014. Its chair had been contacted by "a serving police officer, PC James Patrick, acting as a whistleblower, who had serious concerns about the validity of crime statistics." The committee's summary: "The attitudes and behaviour which lead to the misrecording of crime have become ingrained, including within senior leadership. This leads to the subordination of data integrity to target-chasing."

It deprecated targets "in the strongest possible terms" and called the end of regular external audit in 2007 "a mistake". HMIC called the failure to record "wholly unacceptable". It had looked for "hard evidence of improper practices (such as dishonest manipulation)", and "relatively little was found." A number of forces, it added, accepted that "the culture of chasing targets as ends in themselves had distorted crime-recording decisions".

## The bit continues

Fixing the recording moved the number. In the year ending March 2016 the survey fell 6% and police-recorded crime rose 8%. ONS said "most of the latest rise is thought to be due to improved crime recording practices and processes".

Inspections later put recording accuracy at 80.5% in 2014 and 92.4% at the end of the 2021 to 2022 programme, "a statistically significant change". In May 2024 the Office for Statistics Regulation still listed police-recorded crime as "official statistics, not accredited official statistics", and called its review "the first step towards the statistics being considered for reaccreditation."

The same review noted that the survey was not accredited either, "due to challenges with response rates". The regulator reaccredited the survey in October 2024. ONS's July 2026 bulletin still describes the police figures as "official statistics", not accredited ones.
