## The incident

Overnight on Friday 2 October 2020, Public Health England (PHE) identified 15,841 positive COVID-19 results that had not been included in England's reported daily cases. PHE dated them 25 September to 2 October; 11,968, over 75%, should have been reported between 30 September and 2 October. Everyone affected "received their COVID-19 test result as normal." All outstanding cases were transferred to contact tracing "by 1am on 3 October."

PHE's cause: in the "data load process that transfers COVID-19 positive lab results into reporting dashboards," "some files containing positive test results exceeded the maximum file size." The statement does not use the words Excel, XLS, rows or columns.

## Who lied

Nobody, on the record. The documents disagree about which file.

On 5 October the Health Secretary told the Commons the problem "emerged in a PHE legacy system" and spoke of "a maximum file size error." In Hansard's record of the debate, "Excel" appears only in questions from 2 opposition MPs, including: "Why are critical databases in a national pandemic being hosted on Excel spreadsheets?"

On 8 October Greg Clark, chair of the Science and Technology Committee, wrote to Baroness Dido Harding that the Secretary of State "told the House on Monday 5 October that the issue was related to a Public Health England (PHE) legacy system, which included the use of Microsoft Excel." Then: "It is reported that PHE developers used an old format of the software, XLS, which had a set number of rows to log data that was incompatible with the volume of data being generated."

Harding replied on 12 October: "The technical issue was not caused by the use of an old format." Her account: increased Pillar 2 testing volumes "meant that some Excel files used in the transfer process exceeded their file size threshold and so were not uploaded."

The reply confirms Excel and denies the old format. The old-format story enters the official record after the words "It is reported" and is denied 4 days later.

The smaller figures drift. The reply dates the cases 24 September to 1 October, 1 day earlier than PHE. Hansard has "6,500 hours of extra contact tracing"; the reply has 6,000. Nobody disputes the 15,841.

## The fix

PHE's mitigation "splits large files." The Health Secretary told the House the department had decided in July to replace the legacy system; contracts were "awarded in August." By 9am on 5 October, "51% of the cases have now been contacted a second time for contact tracing purposes." The reply: "As of the morning of 12 October, 79% of people testing positive have been reached and asked for details of their contacts."

The reply also said "A full wash-up of the incident is ongoing." This site found none published. The decommissioned dashboard's archive warns that figures by publish date "may include the publication of a backlog of data". On 15 October the Office for Statistics Regulation wrote that the error "was not directly part of official statistics production" and launched a review of reproducible analytical pipelines.
